Design and Development of a Web-Based Tracer Study System in
Higher Education Using the Waterfall Method (Case Study at Dian
Nusantara University)
Mei Rendy Priyanto1, Desi Ramayanti2
Universitas Dian Nusantara

ABSTRACT

Keywords:
Website,
PHP,
Waterfall,
tracer study

This study aims to design and develop a web-based tracer
study system at Dian Nusantara University using the Waterfall
method. The system is designed to assist higher education
institutions in tracking alumni's career paths and their level of
satisfaction with the education they received. Additionally,
this system aims to expedite the survey form completion
process by alumni, ensuring that the collected data is more
accurate and easier to analyze. The development stages
include requirements analysis, design, implementation, and
testing to ensure system performance. The outcome of this
research is a platform that allows alumni to fill out surveys
online, facilitating data processing by the university. This
system is expected to strengthen the relationship with alumni
and provide valuable information for curriculum development
and educational planning.

INTRODUCTION
The success indicator of higher education is how relevant graduates are to the needs of the job
market. Higher education institutions are expected to produce graduates who are competitive
and ready to contribute to development, both locally and globally. Although many
universities have conducted tracer studies on their graduates, the results have not been
satisfactory. Data from tracer studies are crucial for universities to evaluate the effectiveness
of their education on students. In fact, during grant processes and accreditation, data from
tracer studies, such as the waiting period for graduates, the percentage of graduates who are
employed, and their initial income, are often required. [4]
Currently, Dian Nusantara University (UNDIRA) does not have a dedicated tracer study
application. The alumni data tracking mechanism is done by contacting alumni via email and
attaching a Google Form as a medium for filling out the survey. Although Google Forms is
easy to use and free, there are several limitations that Dian Nusantara University must
consider for the sustainability of its tracer study activities in the coming years.[2]

The proposed solution is the development of a Tracer Study application. The purpose of
developing the tracer study application is to integrate the research needs of the tracer study
into an application. This application offers significant benefits, such as helping study programs
improve their curriculum by providing feedback and preparing the necessary tracer study
documentation for accreditation processes. Through this platform, alumni career tracking can
be done more efficiently and systematically. Alumni can be invited to fill out the survey online,
reducing barriers such as difficulty in contacting them and receiving responses. Additionally,
the web-based tracer study allows for centralized data storage and management, making it
easier for relevant parties, such as study program staff and education decision-makers, to
access and analyze the data. Therefore, creating a web-based tracer study can be an effective
step in improving the accuracy and relevance of information about alumni career paths and
supporting efforts to evaluate and develop educational programs at higher education
institutions . [7]
The main issue faced is the collection of alumni data at Dian Nusantara University, especially
related to the evaluation of graduate success in the information technology industry. Manual
data collection results in difficulties in tracking alumni career paths, low response rates, and
challenges in storing and analyzing data adequately. The proposed solution is the creation of
a web-based tracer study platform that allows for online data collection. With this platform, it
is expected that the process of tracking and collecting alumni data will be more efficient and
structured. Furthermore, centralized data storage and easy access for relevant parties will
facilitate data analysis and decision-making related to the enhancement of educational
programs and curricula .[3]
METHODS
Data Collection
In this study, data collection techniques were conducted using interviews and observations.
The purpose of the interviews was to gather relevant data and information to assist in
observing and analyzing the processes occurring in the tracer study system at Dian Nusantara
University. The data and information used were sourced from books, scholarly articles, and
journals available on the internet. The observation aimed to collect existing data and
information.[1]
Software Development Methods
This information system was developed using the SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle)
model, which includes problem analysis, requirements specification, design, coding, testing,
system integration, and operation. System maintenance and evolution are necessary to ensure
the continued functionality of the system. The advantage of applying the fountain model is
that certain stages can be prioritized or skipped, although some stages, such as
implementation, cannot be prioritized before the design phase. The data collection methods
used were observation and interviews. The system testing results indicate that the mobilebased tracer study information system enables higher education institutions to effectively and
efficiently collect the required information.[5]
Waterfall Method
To carry out the software development process, the method used is the waterfall method. The
Waterfall method is a sequential method of each development part that is owned. The
Waterfall method is a development method that is a Linear Sequential Model which is often
also known as the classic life cycle, starting from the initial part of the system development
specification, namely the planning, modeling, construction and delivery of the system. to users
(deployment), which ends with support for the complete software produced. [6]The
development method uses a waterfall model similar to that in the following picture:

This waterfall method has several steps:
1. Communications
This stage is used to analyze the importance of the tracer study system, as well as the steps
in collecting data and holding meetings with research objects, both to collect additional
data and those contained in journals or articles.
2. Planning
In this planning stage, which is a continuation of the communication stage, the system's
services, constraints, and objectives are developed through consultation with system
users. The description of the services provided by the system and the restrictions specified
in detail serve as a detailed component of the system.
3. Modeling
This stage is a design and modeling stage that aims to translate system requirements,
focusing on designing data structures, software architecture, interface appearance, and
program algorithms. The goal is to gain a better understanding of the overall picture of
what will be done.
4. Construction
In the Construction process, this is the process of translating the design form into a code
or form of language that can be read by a computer. The programmer will translate the
code into a software interface through coding. This process is the real process of handling
software, therefore the use of computers is maximized in this process. After the coding is
complete, testing is carried out on the system and also the code that has been created. The
goal is to find errors that may occur so that they can be corrected later.
5. Deployments
The Deployment process is the stage of implementing the software to users, or it could
also be said to be the final stage in creating the software. After analysis, design and coding
have been carried out, the system that has been created will be used by users. Then the
software that has been created will always be checked periodically, software repaired,
software evaluated, and software developed based on the feedback provided so that the
system can continue to run and develop according to its function.
Unified Modeling Language (UML)
Unified Modeling Language, hereinafter referred to as (UML), is a system development
technique that uses graphical language as a tool for documenting and carrying out system
specifications. In this research, the system design uses UML, which consists of creating use
case diagrams, activity diagrams, and sequence diagrams. System software design includes
database design, producing a mapping of tables depicted with an Entity Relationship
Diagram. [13]
Test Result
System testing uses Blackbox testing, which is a software testing technique that focuses on the
functional specifications of the software without having to pay attention to software details.
Black box testing works by entering data in each form. Black box testing allows software

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Analysis of the tracer study system using Google Forms for collecting alumni data reveals
several weaknesses, including low response rates and difficulties in data management. The
system lacks a reminder feature for alumni who have not completed the form and does not
provide centralized data storage. The development of a web-based application for the tracer
study enables alumni to access a portal and complete the survey online more flexibly, which
is expected to improve response rates. Additionally, all collected data will be stored in a
centralized database, facilitating easier access and analysis. This process can also be monitored
directly by administrators, both in terms of the number of alumni responding and the results
of each survey question.
Analysis
The development of a website for alumni form completion serves as an effective solution to
address the challenges in collecting tracer study data. This website is designed with several
key features, including survey question formulation that adheres to the regulations of the
Ministry of Education and Culture (Kemendikbud) and additional inputs from the university
to enhance the system. The website also includes the creation of an alumni database for
broadcasting needs and data management. The survey mechanism simplifies the process by
tailoring it according to alumni employment status. Additionally, the website is equipped with
a user-friendly tracer study form page, a dashboard for data analysis, and an export function
that complies with Kemendikbud's formatting requirements.

CONCLUSION
The research conducted at Dian Nusantara University reveals that the tracer study information
system still relies on Google Forms. Based on these findings, the author developed a webbased tracer study information system. This system allows alumni to update their current
personal information and complete the tracer study survey more flexibly. The application also
facilitates the tracer study team in tracking and managing alumni data, enabling efficient
survey completion monitoring and simplifying the analysis of survey responses from alumni.
